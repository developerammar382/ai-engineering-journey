# A Short History of the Coffee Bean

Coffee is one of the most popular drinks in the world. Every day, people drink more than two billion cups. But the story of coffee started long ago, in the highlands of Ethiopia.

A popular legend says a goat herder named Kaldi noticed his goats became very energetic after eating red berries from a certain tree. He told a local monk, who made a drink from the berries and found that it kept him awake during long evening prayers. Historians are not sure the story is true, but it shows how people have always linked coffee with energy.

By the 15th century, coffee was grown in Yemen. Coffee houses called "qahveh khaneh" opened in cities across the Middle East. People went there not only to drink coffee but also to listen to music, play chess, and talk about the news. They were sometimes called "schools of the wise".

In the 17th century, coffee reached Europe. Some people were suspicious of the new drink at first, but coffee houses quickly became popular in London, Paris, and Vienna. In London they were called "penny universities", because for the price of one penny you could buy a cup of coffee and join interesting conversations.

The Dutch started growing coffee in their colonies in Asia, and later the French brought coffee plants to the Caribbean. Brazil began growing coffee in the 18th century and today it is the largest coffee producer in the world, followed by Vietnam and Colombia.

There are two main types of coffee beans. Arabica beans have a smoother, sweeter taste and are grown at higher altitudes. Robusta beans are stronger, more bitter, and have almost twice as much caffeine. Most specialty coffee shops use Arabica, while Robusta is common in instant coffee and espresso blends.

Today, coffee is a huge global industry. Millions of farmers depend on it for their income. Climate change is a growing problem, because coffee plants need a stable climate. Scientists are working on new coffee varieties that can survive hotter temperatures, so that future generations can still enjoy their morning cup.
