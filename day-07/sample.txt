Remote work became common for many software teams after 2020. Companies found that
developers could stay productive at home, and many workers enjoyed skipping the daily
commute. At the same time, teams faced new problems. Communication was slower, new
team members found it harder to learn from senior colleagues, and some people felt
lonely working alone every day.

To deal with this, many teams moved to a hybrid model. People work from home most of
the week and meet in the office one or two days for planning, pair programming and
social time. Tools also improved: shared documents, video calls and chat apps made it
easier to work together from different cities.

Research is still mixed. Some studies show remote workers are more productive, while
others show they collaborate less and come up with fewer new ideas. Most experts agree
that there is no single best answer. The right setup depends on the team, the kind of
work, and what the people on the team need.
